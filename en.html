Purpose and permanence

Institutions that endure are not born of a subscription or of a campaign. They are born of a small number of persons who resolve to raise something intended to outlive them. Thus were built the libraries, the observatories and the cloisters that stand to this day: with stone, with patience and with a few names entered in the deed of foundation.

Omnia Sub Sole offers neither return nor ownership. It offers permanence: a stone building conceived to endure for centuries, an archive that preserves the work of those who compose it, and an entity without owners, whose assets may be neither alienated nor divided. Those who join at this stage acquire nothing: they found.

Premises of its own

A permanent seat for work at present dispersed: a library, an archive and study rooms with an address of their own.

Entry in the deed

The first signatories are recorded as founding members in the deed of incorporation, a public and permanent document.

Works, not current expenditure

Contributions are converted into foundations, walls and vaults. Each section built is recorded under the name of the person who made it possible.

Publication and preservation

Studies are published under their author's name, submitted to internal review and preserved in the archive of the Foundation.

A voice in governance

One member of the cloister, one vote, irrespective of the sum contributed. Influence is not acquired with money.

Use of the premises

Access to the library, to the symposia and to the field programmes. At a later phase, residence for those who evidence one year of collaboration.

Express exclusions

  • No financial return and no participation in the assets of the entity.
  • No title to the land, the building or the archive.
  • No place of residence in return for money.
  • No right of veto over what is studied or published.
  • Outside Colombia, contributions confer no entitlement to tax relief.

The Foundation has not been incorporated and receives no financial contributions. Those wishing to take part may register and, should they see fit, sign a letter of commitment; no payment is involved.

Nature and purposes

Omnia Sub Sole is a non-profit foundation in the course of incorporation. Its purpose is to raise and maintain, on the outskirts of Cartagena de Indias, a cloister of deep study and research, furnished with an archive and a library and conceived to endure.

The Foundation has no owners. Whatever it receives or raises, the land, the building and the archive, shall belong to the entity, and its assets may not be distributed among individuals. Upon dissolution they would pass to another non-profit body of similar purposes.

The work of the cloister follows a deliberate order: first, the place we inhabit; then its origin; and, upon that foundation, the manner in which men live together.

Geodesy and observation of the sky

Study of the shape of the Earth and observation of the sky through the critical examination of the models advanced throughout history, including those not accepted by contemporary science, drawing on historical cartography, ancient sources and direct observation.

Cosmogony and natural theology

Study of the origin of the world and of its Creator through the creation accounts of the several cultures, ancient texts and philosophy. It constitutes a line of research: the Foundation is not a church, nor does it require adherence to any creed.

Civilisation and society

Study of the causes of the great ills of human history, war, famine, insecurity, racism and intolerance, and comparative examination of the models of social organisation, with the aim of proposing more just and more lasting models of common life.

Rules of research

The Foundation conceives deep research as rigorous, unhurried and documented work, subject to the following rules:

  • Primary sources. Study proceeds from historical maps, ancient texts and original documents or faithful copies of them.
  • Citation of origin. Every study records its sources, so that any reader may verify them.
  • Internal review. Before publication, each study is reviewed by other members of the cloister.
  • Preservation. A copy of every published study is deposited in the Foundation's archive.
  • Dissemination. Findings are made known through study papers, symposia and lectures.

Programmes of research

The lines of research are supported by three field programmes, conducted with the Foundation's own means.

High-altitude aerial exploration

Balloons of the Foundation's own design, furnished with measuring instruments, high-definition video, real-time transmission and the recording of unidentified aerial phenomena.

Long-distance observation and measurement

Long-distance photography and laser measurement over land and over large bodies of water.

Cartography

Charts drawn from aerial observation, field measurement and historical cartography.

Premises and building

The premises shall be raised in phases on a plot of 1,012 m² in El Rancho I, a country estate developed by San Martín Group in Santa Rosa de Lima, some twenty minutes from Cartagena de Indias, which the founder shall contribute to the entity once it is incorporated and the deed executed. The estate's rules require a 1.5 m setback from boundaries and limit height to two storeys plus a roof terrace; the preliminary design complies with both. The building shall consist of two storeys arranged about a central courtyard, raised wholly in stone.

  • Stone construction. Foundations, walls and stone vaulted roofs, after the building tradition of the fortifications of Cartagena.
  • Rooms. A geodesy room, a cosmogony and natural theology room, a civilisation and society room, a museum, the archive, the library and a residence for resident members.
  • Museum. A permanent exhibition room with historical cartography, instruments and pieces from the archive, open to the public through guided visits.
  • Self-sufficiency. A kitchen garden, solar power upon the roof terrace and an independent water supply.

Floor plans of the preliminary design: some 440 m² built per floor, plus a central courtyard of 54 m². The definitive layout and orientation shall be settled with the architectural project, and the definitive design shall be subject to technical studies, to the building permit and to the rules of the estate.

Forms of participation

Collaboration is subject to no fee whatsoever. The condition of member of the cloister, which carries voice and vote, entails an annual fee fixed by the council. In no case does the sum contributed confer a greater say in decisions.

Study

Research in primary sources, translation and the writing of studies.

Construction

Stonemasonry, bricklaying, carpentry and the remaining building trades.

Archive and library

The cataloguing and preservation of maps, books and documents.

Administration

Accounts, official procedures and regulatory compliance.

Patronage

Financial contributions, once the Foundation is incorporated.

Documentation

Photographic and audiovisual record of the project.

Engineering and electronics

Design of balloons, measuring systems and data transmission.

Cartography

Surveying, aerial photography and the drawing of charts.

Means of support

The Foundation is incorporated with a minimum of ten signatories, who are entered as founding members in the deed. That body is not enlarged; participation, by contrast, remains open in perpetuity, and the cloister is sustained by new members and by sources of income conceived to last as long as the building.

New members of the cloister

Admission remains open: application, a period of collaboration and the approval of the council. Members support the entity through an annual fee.

Donors

Occasional or periodic contributions from those who do not hold the condition of members. Every contribution receives its certificate.

Named work

Each phase yields elements that may bear, as symbolic recognition, the name of whoever funds them: an ashlar, a window or a room.

Study papers

Subscription to the publications of the cloister: the most modest source and the steadiest.

Museum and guided visits

The Foundation's own income from the opening of the premises, distinct from members' fees and from donations.

Bequests

The Foundation accepts bequests, applied wholly to the building and its preservation.

Endurance reserve

A fixed share of every unrestricted contribution is set aside in a reserve not applied to current expenditure. That reserve provides for the preservation of the building and of the archive in those years in which no works or campaign are in progress.

Financial contributions shall be received only once the Foundation is incorporated and holds a bank account of its own.

Current conditions

  • The premises offer no accommodation. The residence shall open at a later phase: whoever evidences a year of collaboration and defrays their own support may be admitted as a resident.
  • No financial contributions are received until the Foundation is incorporated and holds a bank account of its own.
  • The Foundation shall be incorporated once at least ten persons have signed a letter of commitment. Ten is the minimum required, not a limit upon participants.
  • Contributions are not refundable. They are incorporated into the assets of the Foundation.

Clarifications

  • Non-denominational and non-partisan. Cosmogony and natural theology constitute a line of study. The Foundation requires adherence to no creed and takes no part in party politics.
  • Title. The land, the building and the archive shall become the property of the Foundation, and its assets may not be distributed among individuals.
  • Fees. Collaboration costs nothing; only the condition of member of the cloister entails an annual fee. All other contributions are voluntary and shall be received solely in the Foundation's account, once incorporated.
  • Number of founders. Ten is the minimum required to incorporate the entity. Admission of new members and the receipt of donations remain open.
  • Residence. The condition of resident shall open at a later phase, after a year of collaboration, a trial period, an approved plan of work and self-support.
  • Location of the property. El Rancho I, a country estate developed by San Martín Group in Santa Rosa de Lima, some twenty minutes from Cartagena de Indias. The lot number is disclosed to registered applicants.
